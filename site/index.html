The panel

Three readers who each want something different

Each persona is a stake, a set of priorities and a blind spot. Together they disagree, and the disagreement is where the sharper business question hides.

Elena Varga

The Skeptical CFO

The one who signs and presents to the board. Use her for any number that goes up the chain.

“Will this number survive the audit committee?”
Cares about
Does it tie? Is the variance real or mechanical? What decision does it change?
Blind spot
Loves a clean story, and may accept a tidy bridge over a messy truth.

The Group Controller

Ties every number

The one who checks that every number ties. Use them on anything built from many source files.

“If the document does not state the reconciliation, the number is untied.”
Cares about
Every figure sourced, every method stated, estimates never passed off as actuals.
Blind spot
Checks that numbers tie, not whether the analysis answers the business question.

The Red-Team Reviewer

Attacks from outside

The one who attacks from outside finance. Use them on anything the people it describes will challenge.

“Which sentence will the audit-committee chair read aloud after the restatement?”
Cares about
The figure a regional manager will dispute, and the sentence an auditor will quote.
Blind spot
Assumes bad faith and worst cases.

Your request

Tell them what you expect

Tick one, two or all three. Every persona answers every request you pick, each from their own stake.

Criticize

Find what would stop the document, in the persona's own review format, with every objection tied to a quoted sentence.

You get a one-line verdict, the strongest objections, and the question they would ask you in the meeting.

Review for consistency

Hunt for the places where the document disagrees with itself: totals that do not foot, shifting dates and currencies, a term with two meanings.

You get a table of paired quotes that conflict, why each matters, and the one to fix first.

Suggest enrichments

Ask what to add so the document survives its reader: a bridge, a reconciliation, a sensitivity, an owner, a decision.

You get up to six additions, where each goes, and the data you must fetch. Never invented figures.

How it works

Four steps, about a minute

No framework and no agent loop: each review is one persona file plus one call to the model, run in parallel. The chair only combines; it adds nothing of its own.

Drop the document

Markdown is read as is. Word goes through pandoc, Excel sheet by sheet with its formulas listed.

Pick the panel

Tick the personas and the requests. The prompts are the Think_Ask persona files, word for word.

The panel reviews

All reviews run at once. An optional chair combines them and sharpens the business question.

Read the document

A Marp file on page_afp, laid out like AFP2026_Doc_template.md, plus HTML and PDF.

The output

A review document you can hand to someone

Real pages from a review of the workshop's sample forecast memo. Long reviews flow onto as many Letter pages as they need, so nothing runs past the footer.

A divider per personaEach persona's reviews follow their own navy divider, one request per page.
Editable sourceThe Markdown stays yours: change it in VS Code with the Marp preview, export again.

Guardrails

Built to criticize, not to rewrite

The rules come from the persona files and are repeated in every request.

Critique, never rewriteThe personas point at sentences. The author keeps the pen and every number.
No invented figuresNo made-up rates, sources or recomputations. “I would need to check” is an acceptable answer.
Quote the documentEvery objection points at an exact sentence or number, so you can check it.
Blind spots on purposeEach persona states one. When two reviewers disagree, check the source yourself.
Stays on your MacYour document goes nowhere but to the model provider you chose. Reviews are saved on your Mac.
Your model, your keyAnthropic or OpenAI, with your own API key, entered once in Settings and kept on your Mac.

Install

On your Mac in two minutes

One download, no terminal. Everything BoardReady needs is inside the app, including the export to PDF.

1. Download and drag

Open BoardReady.dmg and drag BoardReady onto the Applications folder.

Download BoardReady.dmg

About 100 MB · Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) · macOS 12 or later

2. Open it the first time

BoardReady is not notarized by Apple, so macOS asks once: right-click the app in Applications, choose Open, then Open again. If macOS still refuses, go to System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway.

3. Add your API key

Click Settings, choose Anthropic or OpenAI and a model, and paste your key. It is billed by your provider, separately from a ChatGPT or Claude subscription, and it never leaves your Mac except to call that provider.

4. Drop a document

Or click Try the sample forecast memo. Reviews are saved in Documents › BoardReady as Markdown, HTML and PDF.

For the PDF export: Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge installed. · Workshop folder: facilitators can also run BoardReady from 1. Think_Ask/document review with uv run app.py, where it reads the persona files and .env live.
